# Online Psychoanalysis Framework

A practical framework for building a psychological profile of anyone through their digital footprint.

---

## Phase 1 — Data Collection

Gather everything available: childhood references, family mentions, career timeline, recurring topics, what they post about obsessively, what they conspicuously *never* mention, their humor style, who they admire, who they attack, and how they handle conflict publicly.

---

## Phase 2 — Map the Prediction Machine

The psyche is a prediction engine shaped by childhood. Look for clues about what was punished vs. rewarded early on.

- Someone who constantly signals self-reliance and mocks vulnerability likely learned that needing others was **unsafe**.
- Someone who over-explains and hedges every opinion probably got punished for being wrong.

The default stance toward any life domain is always **avoid / indifference / embrace**. Figure out which mode they default to across key domains:

- Money
- Sex & intimacy
- Status & recognition
- Conflict & confrontation
- Authority & power

Shifting from avoidance to embrace happens through processing and enjoyment — not suppression or "sucking it up."

---

## Phase 3 — Identify the Shadow

The shadow is what someone has disowned. It shows up in two places online:

**What they attack or mock repeatedly.** That's the disowned part of themselves. Someone who relentlessly ridicules "soft" people is often terrified of their own softness. Someone who despises arrogance may be suppressing their own desire for dominance.

**What they conspicuously avoid.** Silence is data. If someone posts about every area of life except relationships, that's where the wound likely sits.

**The nemesis test:** What would *destroy* this person if a competitor did it? That reveals their deepest insecurity — and flipped, their deepest unintegrated desire. The shadow of a bag of shit is a bag of gold. Something in them *wants* the exact thing they dread most.

Remember: shadow isn't only the "dark" side (selfishness, aggression, jealousy). For many people the missed half is the *positive* shadow — empowerment, pleasure, hope, boldness, dominance — qualities they've made unsafe to express.

---

## Phase 4 — Fears and Desires (Two Sides of One Coin)

The biggest fear and the biggest desire are almost always mirrors. Map them as pairs:

| Fear | Desire |
|---|---|
| Irrelevance | Legacy / impact |
| Abandonment | Deep belonging |
| Exposure / shame | Full authenticity |
| Powerlessness | Control / mastery |
| Engulfment / loss of self | True intimacy |
| Failure | Greatness / ambition |
| Rejection | Acceptance as-is |

Online behavior reveals which pair dominates. Overcompensation points to the fear side; longing and aspiration posts point to the desire side.

---

## Phase 5 — Defense Mechanisms

Catalog how they protect themselves. Common defenses visible online:

- **Intellectualization** — turning everything into abstract debate to avoid feeling
- **Humor as deflection** — cracking jokes whenever things get real
- **Projection** — accusing others of exactly what they do
- **Reaction formation** — performing the opposite of what they feel
- **Grandiosity** — inflating the self to cover shame
- **Self-deprecation** — preemptively criticizing themselves before anyone else can
- **Splitting** — people and ideas are all-good or all-bad, no middle ground
- **Withdrawal** — going silent or disappearing when triggered

---

## Phase 6 — Mind / Heart / Gut Alignment Check

Mind, heart, and gut are almost completely different entities with different priorities, beliefs, and desires. Look for contradictions between:

- **Mind** — stated beliefs, intellectual positions, rational arguments
- **Heart** — emotional reactions, what triggers them, what moves them
- **Gut** — actual behavior, life choices, instinctive actions

Misalignment is where the real story lives. Someone who intellectually champions risk-taking but lives an extremely safe life has a heart or gut override they haven't faced. When all three vectors point in different directions, outcomes feel random — like rolling dice.

---

## Phase 7 — Recurring Relational Patterns

How do they relate to authority figures, peers, and people below them? Look for:

- Idealize then devalue cycles
- Constant approval-seeking
- Preemptive rejection (leaving before being left)
- Dominance / submission defaults
- Rescuer / victim dynamics
- Merging (losing themselves in others) vs. rigid independence

These patterns replay childhood dynamics and are highly visible in how someone engages in comment sections, with followers, with collaborators, and with public figures they admire or despise.

---

## Phase 8 — Synthesize the Core Wound

Everything above should converge on one or two core wounds — the foundational experiences that shaped their prediction machine. Common core wounds:

- Not being **seen**
- Not being **safe**
- Not being **enough**
- Not being **in control**
- Not being **loved as-is**
- Not being **allowed to exist fully**

The core wound generates the shadow, the defenses, the fear/desire pairs, and the relational patterns. Name it clearly.

---

## How to Use This

Don't just analyze. Analysis alone is the trap — it creates the illusion of progress without movement. The profile is only useful if it answers one question:

> **What is this person avoiding that, if they turned toward it, would make them fundamentally more whole?**

Full engagement beats endless diagnosis. The point isn't to label someone — it's to see what integration would look like for them and where the split lives.
